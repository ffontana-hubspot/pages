What we've heard

Your world, as we understand it.

Harry the Hirer is one of Australia's most established event and exhibition services businesses — fifty years in market, seven specialist divisions, and a national footprint. That scale brings real opportunity, and the conversations across the past few weeks have surfaced where the current setup is starting to creak.

~500
Salespeople

Across Melbourne, Sydney and Brisbane — all working without a shared view of the client.

7
Sales divisions

Expo, Signage, AV, Productions, Furniture, Marquees and Structures — selling into each other's clients, often without realising it.

2
Main offices

Melbourne and Sydney — and the duplicate-quoting risk that comes with two regions chasing the same brief.

70/30
B2B / B2C split

Mostly corporate exhibitions, conferences and activations — with a steady IT-department wedding pipeline alongside.

The friction

What's getting in the way today.

None of this is news to the team — but it's worth naming clearly, because every part of the HubSpot case below responds directly to one of these.

01

Quoting against ourselves

Sydney and Melbourne are pricing the same client, twice, with no shared view to catch it. Someone in one office has already quoted a major exhibition, but a colleague in the other has no way of knowing — until the client mentions it and the experience starts to feel disjointed.

02

New business lives everywhere except a CRM

Harry's One handles existing clients well, but prospects and new opportunities sit across SharePoint folders, spreadsheets and inboxes. The rest lives in people's heads — reminders set, follow-ups pencilled in four or five months out, with no shared system to back it up.

03

Seven divisions, no shared lens

Expo wins a major exhibition; AV and Production don't always hear about it. Signage has a long-standing relationship Marquees would love to know about. The cross-sell opportunity across divisions is real, but right now it's invisible. Fifty years in market, and clients still don't know all the services Harry's offers.

04

No pipeline, no lost-reason data

Today there's no way to see what's coming, what stage every deal is sitting in, or — when something is lost — why. No forecasting, no sales report, no analytical view on what's slipping away. Decisions get made on instinct because the data simply isn't there to support them.

What good looks like

One client record, every division, every conversation.

Each of these is achievable, and similar multi-divisional businesses have already got there. They're the measuring sticks we'd want to hold ourselves to together — and they map directly to the points raised on the discovery call.

01

One shared client record across every division

Whoever owns a major exhibition account in Sydney can see exactly what colleagues in Melbourne are quoting. The duplicate-quote problem dies the day this goes live — and every division gets the context of every other conversation that's happening with that client.

02

A sales pipeline that mirrors how deals actually move

From first enquiry through proposal, to won or lost, with reasons captured for every loss — so the business gets smarter over time. The leadership team finally has a forecast view, and lost-reason data that informs how Harry's competes next time.

03

Cross-divisional mining, made obvious

The AV team filters the pipeline for any Expo win that doesn't have AV attached. One click, one bulk-personalised campaign, every opportunity surfaced. The same trick works for Signage looking at Marquees, Productions looking at Furniture — every direction.

04

Proactive nurture against the events calendar

Annual exhibitions ping the account manager six months out: "Last year you supported BuildEx with the booth and the AV — same again?" The conversation starts before the competition's even thought about it. Recurring events stop being a memory test and start being a system.

05

Mobile-first for the road

Business card scans, voice-to-text notes, AI in-person meeting notetakers, click-to-call from the venue. Everything we walked through in the demo — built for AMs out at sites and exhibitions, not chained to a desk. The phone in their pocket becomes their main CRM input device.

The solution

One platform. Three moving parts, working as one.

Each piece handles a distinct part of the day-to-day — and because they share the same client record, there's no double-handling, no data re-entry, and no division working in the dark about another's conversations.

The shared client record
For every account manager, every division

One company, one timeline, every division's activity visible. Outlook plugs in directly so day-to-day stays familiar — AMs work where they already are, and the CRM catches up in the background.

  • Outlook integration so AMs work from their inbox
  • One company record, every division's activity visible
  • AI enrichment to fill in industry, size and segment automatically
  • Granular permissions to lock down sensitive divisions where needed
  • Mobile app with business-card scan and voice-to-text logging
The deal pipeline
For the leadership team and every sales manager

Deals, stages, lost reasons, forecast. Everything that's missing today, in one place. Filter on what's not attached to a deal to surface cross-sell opportunities in seconds — exactly the move we walked through in the demo.

  • Configurable deal stages per division
  • Lost-reason capture for compliance and learning
  • Cross-divisional filter views (e.g. all Expo deals without AV)
  • Sales pipeline and forecast dashboards
  • Email templates and sequences for bulk personalised outreach
The growth engine
For the wider business, when ready

Reporting, automation, and the annual events calendar that turns past jobs into future revenue. A phased rollout means starting with Expo and Signage, learning what works, then layering in the other divisions at your pace.

  • Activity dashboards by rep, division and state
  • Goal trackers against revenue and meeting targets
  • Automated reminders against recurring annual events
  • Lost-deal analytics and pipeline forecasting
  • Scheduled dashboard emails to leadership
